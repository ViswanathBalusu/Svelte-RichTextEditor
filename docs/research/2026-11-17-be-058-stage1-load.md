# Stage 1 load test of one node (BE-058)

Date of the runs: 2026-11-17 and 2026-11-18. Harness: `services/document-service/scripts/load/be-058.ts` (command), `stage1.ts` (scenario and gate), `lib.ts` (the client of FND-009). Its checks are in `services/document-service/test/load/be-058.harness.test.ts`. The reports of the runs are in `docs/research/data/be-058-*.json`.

## How and where these were measured

- One development machine: Intel Core i9-10900KF (20 logical CPUs), 128 GiB, Windows 11, Node.js 22.18. It is not the reference machine of ADR 0037 and not a cloud region. The service process ran with Node.js's default heap limit.
- The service ran as its own process and the load generator as another, on the same machine, over the loopback interface. "Within one region" is met only in the weakest sense: there is no network distance.
- **The machine was shared.** Other agents ran builds and tests on it while these runs went. The load on it was not recorded. Runs of the same settings differ a great deal, as the tables show (the first memory runs of the day gave a median of 14 to 36 ms at 50 typists; the run in the table gave 4 ms). A figure below is one run, not a distribution.
- Two storage settings: memory (no database), and PostgreSQL 17.11 with the S3-compatible store of the integration-test environment (FND-010), both in Podman on the same machine. The PostgreSQL settings that bear on the run are in the report (`database`): `max_connections` 300, `shared_buffers` 256MB, `synchronous_commit` on, `fsync` on, `wal_sync_method` fdatasync, `wal_level` replica, `checkpoint_timeout` 5min, `max_wal_size` 1GB. A figure under PostgreSQL includes the Podman machine's loopback relay. There was no synchronous standby.
- Commands, from the repository root (`pwsh -NoProfile -File infra/test-env.ps1 up` for PostgreSQL):

```
node services/document-service/scripts/load/be-058.ts --storage memory --out be-058-memory.json
node services/document-service/scripts/load/be-058.ts --storage postgres --out be-058-postgres.json
node services/document-service/scripts/load/be-058.ts --storage postgres --baseline be-058-postgres.json
```

## The workload

- 2,000 connections on 500 documents (the stage 1 targets of 06 §6). The first connection of each document may type; the other three watch. Every connection is opened with `hello` and `ch.open`, and answers the heartbeat as 05 §4 says.
- Phases of 20 s with 25, 50, 75, 100 and 150 typists, each on a document of its own, each sending 3 commits a second ("a few", 06 §10). The specification does not say how many of the 500 open documents have a busy typist, so the number of typists is a setting and the phases show the curve. The phase the gate reads is 50 typists (150 commits a second); that is a setting, chosen from the first runs.
- In each phase, 10 documents of 680,595 bytes of CDM (under the 1 MB of the target) are opened cold on new connections, one at a time, while the typists type.
- At the end every client leaves and the harness reads `dep_open_rooms` once a second for up to 90 s.
- The service's `/metrics` gives the open rooms, the `slow_consumer` count, the delay of the event loop (since the previous scrape, so one phase) and the CPU and memory of the process. The event-loop budget is a setting: 300 ms for the 99th percentile, the 99th-percentile target for acknowledgement, because a stall above that makes the target unreachable by itself.

## What the runs say, and what they do not

- **One node held 2,000 connections on 500 documents** in all four full runs: no connection failed, no healthy client was closed, nothing was closed with `slow_consumer`, and every room closed after the clients left. The number of open rooms matches the documents (500 at the peak).
- **With memory storage the targets are met up to 100 typists (300 commits a second) in one run, and not at 150.** In the single-phase runs of the loaded machine they were missed at 75 typists. The runs cannot say which of those is the node and which the machine.
- **With PostgreSQL the targets for acknowledgement and cold open are not met at the 50-typist gate phase in either run** and are met at 25 typists in one of two runs. With 140 commits a second the service used about 60% of one core and the median acknowledgement was over 100 ms, so the time is in the write path to PostgreSQL (through the Podman relay), not in the service's CPU. The cause was not investigated here. It matches the finding of FND-009 for the idle node (median 58 to 72 ms on PostgreSQL), and belongs to the stories that own the persist pipeline (BE-024, SYN-040) and to a rerun on the reference machine with a real network and disk.
- **Opening a room cold under load is slower than idle.** On PostgreSQL the 95th percentile was 279 to 1,205 ms at 25 typists and up to 7.9 s at 75 typists, against 243 ms for an idle node (FND-009).
- **Later phases are worse than the same load straight after the ramp-up** (memory: 75 typists, 8.6 ms median in the sequence, 47.8 ms alone, but the alone-run was on the loaded machine, so this is not a clean comparison). The service's resident memory grew in every phase, from about 410 MiB to 1,500 MiB over five phases of 20 s on memory storage. The growth cannot be assigned to commits: each phase also opens 10 documents of 680 kB of CDM that stay open until their idle timer, and memory storage keeps everything. Its cause was not investigated; BE-084 owns memory per room.
- The ramp-up of 2,000 connections stalls the event loop (99th percentile 275 to 1,070 ms across the runs, read after the ramp-up). The budget applies to the phases, not to the ramp-up.
- The harness itself needed a fix to hold 2,000 connections for more than 50 s: a watcher that never sends anything is closed by the service after two heartbeat intervals (05 §4), because the service has no reason to ping a connection it keeps writing to. The load client now sends `ping` after a heartbeat interval of its own silence, as 05 §4 says a side does. The FND-009 runs held connections for seconds and did not meet this.
- Not measured: a node of a stated size on the reference machine, a network, a second location, a synchronous standby, memory per room (BE-084), export load.

## The gate

`be-058.ts --baseline <report>` compares the run with an earlier report of the same command and exits 1 when the gate fails (checked: with a tolerance of -99 % the command exits 1 and names the four latencies). The gate fails when:

- a hard check of the run fails: the gate phase was not reached, a client was closed or told `slow_consumer`, a commit was not acknowledged, a connection failed, the open rooms did not return to zero, the event loop was over its budget in the gate phase, or the cold-open document was 1 MB or more; or
- the median or 99th percentile of the acknowledgement, the 95th percentile of the cold open, or the 99th percentile of the event-loop delay of the gate phase is above the baseline by more than the tolerance (50 % by default) and more than the floor (10 ms by default).

The gate does not apply the figures of 00 §9: a machine that misses them passes while it does not get worse than its own baseline. The report says separately, per phase, which targets were met.

The nightly job is not set up. `.github/` is root configuration and was not edited for this story, and a baseline is only meaningful on one machine: the reference runner of ADR 0037 does not exist yet (see `.github/workflows/perf.yml`). When it does, the job runs the third command above against a baseline taken on that runner.
