# 0412. The signing service and the seal step of finalisation

**Status:** proposed, 2026-11-18. Written while building FRW-052 and FRW-053 (lane unblock) on the spike's code (ADR 0411). 12 §8.2 step 3 says a separate signing service seals the PDF and says little else. The decisions below are the lane's. The owner has not seen this record. The production library, key location, certificate and time-stamp authority are not decided (owner decisions 221 to 223) and nothing here chooses them.

1. **Ports.** The service is built over four ports so that each production choice can be swapped: `KeyDirectory` and `KeyService` (the key and its signing call; a digest in, a signature out), `CertificateSource` (the sealing certificate and its issuers for a key identifier), `TimestampSource` (an RFC 3161 client) and a `validationData` function (certificates, CRLs, OCSP responses). The development and test implementations are SoftHSM2 in Podman, a test chain, a local OpenSSL authority and static CRLs.
2. **Wire.** `POST /v1/seal` with a bearer credential and a JSON body (`tenant`, `documentId`, `seq`, `pdfSha256`, `compileSha256`, `seal`, `pdf` in base64); `GET /healthz`; no other route (`services/signing-service/src/http.ts`). The answer is the sealed bytes, their hash, the level, the time of the time-stamp token and the key used, or an error code with `retryable`. This is a proposal: the specification names no protocol between the two services. JSON with base64 costs a third more bytes than a binary body; a binary body is the obvious change if size matters.
3. **Authentication.** The service holds the SHA-256 of each caller's secret, not the secret. Only the caller named `document-service` may seal. A worker's credential is a known credential and is refused with `wrong_caller`. Mutual TLS or a signed token would be stronger; which one the deployment uses is not decided.
4. **Binding.** The request names a document, a `seq` and the PDF's hash. The bytes must hash to that value, both compile hashes (EXP-079's pair) must equal it, and a document and `seq` is bound to one hash for the life of the process. The binding is in memory and a restart forgets it. A store for it is the deployment's.
5. **Rate limit.** A fixed window per caller. The limit and the window are required settings with no default: the specification gives no figure.
6. **Audit.** Each request is written before any key is used (`seal.requested`), and each outcome after (`seal.sealed`, `seal.refused`, `seal.failed`). The service refuses to seal when the audit write fails. Entries hold identifiers, hashes and codes. Where the entries are kept (a write-once store, a log agent) is the deployment's: the service takes a sink. Owner decision 31 of 2026-11-15 keeps signing evidence as long as the signed document; the audit log of the service is not that evidence and no retention is set here.
7. **Level.** `finalisation.seal` is free text in the schema. The service knows `pades-b-lt` and `pades-b-lta` and refuses any other value. A workflow that names none is sealed at B-LT (owner decision 15 of 2026-11-15). The example in 12 §6.1 names `pades-b-lta`.
8. **Whose clock.** The `/M` entry of the signature is the signing service's clock. The seal's time is the time-stamp token's (`genTime`), from the authority's clock. Every other time (the transition, its audit entry, the control commit) is the document service's. ADR 0411's report asked 12 §8.2 to say whose clock `/M` is; this is the proposal.
9. **Failure and retry.** The service gives `tsa_unreachable`, `signing_unavailable` and `audit_unavailable` as retryable and the rest as not. `sealAndFinalise` (`services/document-service/src/workflow/seal-and-finalise.ts`) retries the retryable ones inside one run, with the number of attempts and the delays given by the caller. No default is built in, because the specification states no policy (Open questions 26). When the attempts are used up it leaves the workflow `completed`, writes `workflow.finalisation_failed` and calls an `alert` port. Nothing schedules a later run, and no channel delivers the alert.
10. **Seal party text.** The signature's `/Reason` says the seal is the operator's or the tenant's and not the signature of any signer. The signature has no `/Name` or contact entry.
11. **Shared export.** `selectSealingKey` and `checkSealRequest` (packages/authority) are now exported from the package index. They were only reachable by a relative path.
12. **Not built.** A Containerfile for the service (no Node.js base image is pinned, and the workspace packages need a staged build context as the export worker's image has), the deployment's network boundary, a persistent binding store, a production key service adapter, OCSP, and the alert channel.
