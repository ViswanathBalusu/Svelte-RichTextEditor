# 0385. Correcting a workflow and resuming it: the lock, the marks and the comparison

**Status:** proposed, 2026-11-18. Written while building FRW-032 (lane w4). 12 §6.2 gives the words and leaves these open. Each item takes the smallest reading that works; the owner may change any of them.

1. **The lock of `correcting`.** `lockFor('correcting')` is `{ documentState: 'active', workflowActive: false }`, as for a draft. The owner then changes the body and the fields as an editor of a draft does (12 §4.3: the attribute rule of the outcome check asks for no running workflow). The cost: a whole-document restore and a replacement of the content are not refused with `workflow_active` while the workflow is `correcting`. Both change the body, so `resume` resets everyone who had completed, and no signature stays over a body it did not sign. `lockFor('finalised')` and `lockFor('expired')` stay undecided.
2. **A paused participant.** The room refuses everything that a participant of a `correcting` workflow sends, and that the outcome check refuses, as `read_only` (`isPaused` in `room/room.ts`). A participant is a connection whose `participant` names a participant of the room's workflow record. A user who is not a participant of the workflow keeps the refusals they had. In the service the token's `wfp` is not yet mapped to the connection's `participant` (FRW-038), so a participant token does not reach this path through the service; the room's behaviour is tested with the connection naming its participant.
3. **The marks while `correcting`.** The resource keeps one `state` word for each participant. `correct` writes `paused` for every participant who has not completed and leaves `completed` on one who has, because the resource has no other place to remember who had completed. The participant's own status word, the one the channel carries, is `paused` for every participant while the workflow is `correcting` (`participantStatusWord`), so "every participant is paused" holds for what a participant sees.
4. **The comparison.** The body is the canonical JSON of the document with the `value` of each form field taken out; a field value is the canonical JSON of its value; both are read from the document rebuilt from the log at `sentSeq` and at the head, whole, not as the caller may read it (`snapshotOf`). The owners of a field are the participants who hold its assignee role (`fieldOwnersOf`): a changed field resets the completed participants who hold the role; a field with no assignee, or whose role nobody holds, is treated as a change of the body. A role held by two participants resets both.
5. **After the reset.** A reset participant loses the mark `completed` and the `completedSeq`; the marks are then written again by order (`activeParticipantIds`): the active ones are `active`, the rest `pending`, and a participant who had completed and was not reset stays `completed`. `participant_activated` is raised for each participant who is active again, because each was paused. No event is raised for the resume itself: 12 names no `resumed` webhook. What happens to a reset participant's earlier signature value and its evidence is not touched, as before (12 Open questions).
6. **`sentSeq` stays.** `resume` does not move `sentSeq` or `sentHash`: 12 says the service compares with `sentSeq`, and the send's point in the log is evidence. A second correction therefore compares with the first send and may reset more participants than needed, never fewer. Moving the baseline to the head at `resume` would avoid that; it is the owner's to decide.
7. **Method and path.** `POST /documents/{id}/workflow/correct` and `/resume`, as `send` (G95: assumed). Both need `manage` and take an `Idempotency-Key`. A workflow that is not `in_progress` (for `correct`) or not `correcting` (for `resume`) is `conflict`.
